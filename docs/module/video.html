What happens on screen (text description)

The lecture is a filmed classroom session in which the visual demonstrations carry information that no transcript can convey. This section describes what viewers see, chapter by chapter, and links to the HTML slide deck, which reproduces the on-screen content as text with long descriptions. This is a text alternative, not a recorded audio description.

  1. Goals of the Course (0:27–4:53). The lecturer at the chalkboard; no demo visuals.
  2. Vision, Memory, Attention (4:53–20:09). Projected slides with classic visual illusions: the Müller-Lyer lines, the Ebbinghaus circles, Shepard’s “Terror Subterra” corridor, Adelson’s checker-shadow, and a series of lightness illusions. Then an in-class experiment: two groups receive different verbal labels (“trained seal act” vs. “costume ball”) for the same ambiguous sketch, see it briefly, and answer ten yes/no memory questions — showing how suggestion shaped what was remembered. A selective-attention video (people passing a basketball) is played; most viewers miss a person in a gorilla suit. See slides 8–14 and 16–23.
  3. Hearing, Learning, Memory (20:09–29:58). The McGurk effect: a video plays the syllable “BA” dubbed onto lips saying “GA”; nearly everyone hears “DA.” See slides 25–26.
  4. The Power and Peril of Automaticity (29:58–37:09). Reading demonstrations (“ONE WAY NOT DO ENTER,” “PARIS IN THE THE SPRING”), a count-the-F’s sentence, and the Stroop task with color-word sets — all shown as projected slides. See slides 35–41.
  5. How We Feel vs. How We Act (37:09–49:30). Affective forecasting (tenure, lottery, accident examples), then two bar charts from Kawakami et al. (Science, 2009) comparing predicted vs. actual responses to racist comments. The chart data are available as tables: distress chart data and partner-choice chart data. See slides 43–51.

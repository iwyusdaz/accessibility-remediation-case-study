Prioritization model: remediating a course catalog at scale

How one audited module scales to ~300 courses without pretending the hours don't exist. Web version of program/prioritization-model.md. Markdown source.

Context. Lakeside State University — a fictional US public university preparing for ADA Title II digital accessibility compliance — must bring ~300 online courses toward WCAG 2.1 AA conformance. The DOJ rule requires WCAG 2.1 Level AA for web content and mobile apps of state and local government entities (including public universities); the compliance deadline for large covered public entities, originally April 24, 2026, was extended on April 20, 2026 to April 26, 2027 (requirements unchanged). Sources verified 2026-10-25: Purdue Innovative Learning, "DOJ extends ADA Title II digital accessibility deadlines" and CSU Stanislaus, "Title II Rule: Digital Accessibility" (which also summarizes the archived-content and pre-existing-document exceptions).

One module was remediated in this repository as the pilot. This model is for scaling the same process across the catalog.

1. The scoring model: (Impact + Exposure) ÷ Effort

Each course gets three sub-scores (1–3 each) based on registrar and LMS data:

DimensionQuestionScore 1Score 2Score 3
ImpactWho is blocked, and how badly?Elective, small enrollmentMixed enrollmentRequired course, gateway/large enrollment, or known accommodation requests
ExposureHow often is it touched?Archive-only (rarely visited)Login-only, term-time usePublic-facing pages or weekly high-traffic use
EffortHow much human work per item?Low: under 10 hours estimated (text-heavy HTML, few simple PDFs, no media)Medium: 10–49 hours estimated (some PDFs, short videos)High: 50+ hours estimated (lecture-capture hours, scanned PDFs, many images)

One formula, used everywhere in this document: Priority score = (Impact + Exposure) ÷ Effort. Fix the highest score first: the most students touched for the least effort. Effort divides rather than adds because capacity is the binding constraint, and the Effort sub-score is calibrated in estimated human hours (from the benchmarks in §2) so the worked ranking below is checkable. Ties are broken by the higher (Impact + Exposure), then by the larger enrollment.

Media effort dominates. Benchmarks (cited below): one hour of video ≈ 4–5 hours of human caption correction; one complex PDF page ≈ up to 1 hour of human remediation. So a course with 20 lecture hours and 200 scanned PDF pages is a multi-hundred-hour project regardless of its Impact score — it needs triage sequencing, not just enthusiasm.

Ranks 4–6 tie at 2.0; the tie-break (higher I+E first) puts PSY-100 (6) ahead of HIST-140 and ART-105 (4 each). The formula puts ENGL-115 above BIOL-101 because BIOL's ~26 hours of caption/PDF work outweigh its larger enrollment — per hour of team capacity, ENGL-115 reaches more students.

4. Sequencing recommendation

  1. Fix-forward first (weeks 1–2): new content is created accessibly. Publish the faculty quick guide and make the accessibility checker part of course-copy QA. This stops the meter.
  2. High-impact, low-effort live courses next (weeks 3–8): ranks 1–3 (ENGL-115, BIOL-101, MGMT-305) — each is days-to-weeks of work for hundreds of students per year.
  3. High-effort, high-impact courses (months 3–12): PSY-100 (rank 4) and NURS-210 (rank 7) — budget vendor captioning for the video hours (in-house at 4–5 hr/hr does not fit the timeline), convert scanned PDFs to HTML instead of tagging scans. Ranks 5–6 (HIST-140, ART-105) are medium-effort and slot into normal term work.
  4. Archive courses last: verify the archived-content exception applies (created before the compliance date, unchanged, kept solely for reference/research/recordkeeping), document the exemption per item, and plan retirement. ARCH-490 (rank 8) is parked, not scheduled.
  5. Flag specialists early: math notation (STAT-200) and programming content (CS-101) need skills this playbook does not cover; schedule them with the right experts rather than guessing.

Review cadence: re-score every term; accommodation requests from the disability services office override the model immediately.
